# Eudora: extending teachers' presence with Maya

**Concept case study · Working draft · 1 October 2026**

Eudora explores on-demand explanations and interactive study supported by teachers’ prepared knowledge, including when a student is revising outside class hours.

**Actual human teachers create and curate the education.** Maya extends their availability; Eudora connects that support with student practice and insights for teachers and guardians.

## When students need their teachers

A student's need for help does not always fit a class schedule. A question may arise while revising at home, working through an exercise, or preparing for an exam the next morning. The teacher may already have the right explanation or resource, but cannot personally respond to every student at every moment.

Consider a student revising the night before an exam who becomes stuck on a topic. In the intended Eudora experience, the student can ask for help and receive a response supported by the teacher's curated knowledge, together with a relevant learning element to work through interactively. This is an illustration of the concept; the exact conversation experience remains to be developed.

## One teacher, individual support for many students

Students do not all need the same help. One may need an explanation, another may need further practice, and another may need to revisit an earlier concept. The intended experience connects each student's question with relevant elements from the curated knowledge base.

Individual support should connect each learner to the right explanation or practice activity, while leaving the teacher able to review that learner’s progress.

## An environment for interactive education

Eudora gives teachers an environment for creating interactive learning materials. Those materials become part of the support available to students, connecting explanations with opportunities to participate and practise.

For students, learning becomes an active experience. They can engage with material, ask questions, and work through their understanding. The particular formats for these interactions will be shaped through further discussion.

The specific activity formats and how they connect to a student’s questions remain open design decisions.

## Understanding strengths and weaknesses

Eudora also gives teachers and guardians ways to understand a student's learning more deeply. The concept includes monitoring progress and analysing strengths and weaknesses, helping them see where a student is confident and where further support may be useful.

Teachers can use these insights to inform their teaching and the material they curate. Guardians gain visibility and control within the learning environment so they can participate more meaningfully in the student's education.

The specific guardian controls, available analyses, and presentation of progress are still open design questions.

## A continuing review loop

The quality of Eudora's educational support is intended to keep improving through review. Responses, learning materials, and feedback provide opportunities for educators to identify what needs clarification, correction, or enrichment.

A useful way to express this concept is:

1. Teachers create and curate knowledge and interactive learning material.
2. Students receive individual support and engage with that material.
3. Feedback and learning insights inform educator review.
4. Educators refine the knowledge and learning experience for future use.

Educator review determines which corrections and additions become available in future learning interactions.

## Clio: an assistant shaped by educators

Maya also powers Clio, Eudora's mascot and an ever-learning assistant. Clio can bring a teacher's study material into a student conversation, connecting the learner with education created and curated by actual human teachers.

Clio complements direct teacher conversations and the individual teacher’s persona by helping students find and use relevant study material.

Clio actively learns from feedback curated by a community of educators.

The detailed ways students move between direct teacher conversations, teacher personas, and Clio remain to be discussed.

## What Eudora illustrates about Maya

Eudora applies Maya’s private relationship continuity to individual learners. Interactive materials and guardian insights are education-specific application features; they would need their own scope, access rules, and validation before implementation.

The relationship between direct teacher support, a teacher’s persona, and Clio remains a design question. This concept case study describes their intended roles without claiming a released education product.
